CELL064 Degradation Model — Key Findings (2026-09-20)

Scope: three findings from the CELL064 (GMFEB23S NMC622/Si-Gr pouch cell) PyBaMM degradation-model investigation, covering (1) why DMA-consistent LLI/LAM scaling matters for RPT-shape fidelity, (2) the post-knee porosity-gated SEI-induced isolation mechanism that drives LAM, and (3) why the Beginning-of-Life (BoL) solid-diffusion parameterization (Si particle radius and diffusivity) controls whether the model's End-of-Life (EoL) stoichiometric range matches reality.

All model/code references are to packages/pybamm/src/pybamm/models/submodels/ (core PyBaMM) and packages/pybamm/si_gr_expansion_precursor/pouch_cell_064/degradation_test_matrix/cell064_degradation_fit.py (the CELL064-specific fit driver), current as of this session.

Finding 1: DMA-consistent LLI/LAM scaling is critical for RPT4/RPT5 shape fidelity

The model's total capacity fade at any point in life is the sum of two independent degradation modes: LLI (lithium inventory lost to SEI/side reactions) and LAM (active material lost, mainly Si via the porosity- isolation mechanism, Finding 2). A given total SoH can be reached by very different LLI/LAM splits — and the split matters far beyond the aggregate SoH number, because LLI and LAM shift the discharge curve's shape differently:

  • LLI shifts the whole anode/cathode stoichiometry window relative to each other (a lithium-inventory problem) — it mostly shifts the discharge curve along the voltage axis and shortens it roughly proportionally.
  • LAM (specifically Si LAM) removes an entire phase's contribution to the composite anode's OCP and capacity — it changes the discharge curve's shape, not just its length, because the remaining electrode is a different (more graphite-dominated) composite than before.

The old baseline had proportionally more LLI than Si LAM relative to what the real CELL064 DMA/eSOH fits show (CELL064_LAM_derived.csv: real LAM_Si reaches 58.5% by RPT4/EFC~171 and 79.6% by RPT5/EFC~197 — a large, dominant contribution). With too little LAM_Si and too much LLI, the old baseline's RPT4/RPT5 discharge curves end up shaped more like a lithium-inventory-limited curve than the real, LAM-Si-dominated one — visible directly as a shape mismatch (not just a magnitude mismatch) in both the raw V(Q) curve and its dV/dQ signature (dV/dQ is far more shape-sensitive than V(Q) itself, since it amplifies curvature differences that are hard to see by eye on the raw discharge curve).

The new baseline re-tunes SI_K_SEI_MULT/GR_K_SEI_MULT/BoL porosity together (see Findings 2 and 3) so the model's LLI/LAM_Si split tracks the real DMA-derived split much more closely across life, not just the aggregate SoH-vs-EFC curve.

Update: the old baseline's exact config turned out to be reconstructable after all (see the note above) — build_true_old_baseline.py reproduces it with zero env var overrides plus one debugged stability fix (NEG_POROSITY_FLOOR 0.023->0.028, MAX_CYCLES 250->350), reproducing the documented knee EFC of 107.7 exactly and reaching a natural stop rather than crashing. This makes a genuine 3-way (real / true old / new) comparison possible for the RPT4/RPT5 curves.

Finding 2: post-knee LAM comes from porosity-gated, SEI-induced isolation

Mechanism, in words

Silicon's large volume swing on lithiation/delithiation, combined with SEI growth on cracked/exposed surface, progressively closes the negative electrode's pore network. As porosity falls, at some point remaining electrolyte-filled pathways become so constricted that pockets of active material lose electronic/ionic access — they become electrically isolated and stop contributing to charge/discharge, i.e. Loss of Active Material (LAM). This is modelled as a gate — a smooth, porosity-headroom-dependent function — that ramps a fraction of the ongoing SEI-forming reaction current from "counts toward SEI growth/LLI" to "counts toward LAM instead", staying small pre-knee (deliberately, so it doesn't perturb the already-tuned pre-knee fit) and growing sharply as the electrode's porosity approaches its floor (post-knee).

Equations

Implemented in src/pybamm/models/submodels/active_material/loss_active_material.py (get_coupled_variables, active whenever a phase's "loss of active material" option string contains "porosity", e.g. Si's "reaction-driven and porosity isolation"):

1. Porosity headroom fraction (1 at as-built porosity, 0 at the floor):

fheadroom = clamp( (εn − εn,floor) / (εn,0 − εn,floor) , 0, 1 ) 

headroom_frac = clip((eps_n - eps_floor) / (eps_n0 - eps_floor), 0, 1)

where εn (eps_n) is the electrode's current (structural) porosity, εn,0 (eps_n0) its as-set-up (BoL) value, εn,floor (eps_floor) the parameterised porosity floor ("Negative electrode porosity floor").

2. Isolation-gate target (algebraic, porosity-dependent):

gtarget = (1 − fheadroom)η 

isolation_gate_target = (1 - headroom_frac) ^ eta_LAM_iso

η (eta_LAM_iso, "...porosity-isolation LAM exponent") sets the knee's sharpness/width — a small headroom fraction (near the floor) drives the target toward 1 (full isolation), large headroom fraction keeps it near 0.

3. Isolation-gate state (a lagged ODE state, not an instantaneous snapshot of the target — added so the gate can't jump discontinuously if the target itself has a kink):

dg/dt = (gtarget − g) / τ 

d(isolation_gate)/dt = (isolation_gate_target - isolation_gate) / tau_LAM_iso

τ (tau_LAM_iso, "...porosity-isolation gate time constant [s]") is the relaxation time constant (set_rhs), and g (isolation_gate) is the gate state itself.

4. Remaining-active-fraction self-limiter (so the isolation rate decays toward 0 as the phase is consumed, instead of driving the active volume fraction through/past zero):

fremain = clamp( εs / εs,0 , 0, 1 ) 

remaining_frac = clip(eps_solid / eps_solid_init, 0, 1)

where εs (eps_solid) is the phase's current active-material volume fraction and εs,0 (eps_solid_init) its BoL value.

5. Isolation-driven active-material loss rate. Two implementations exist, selected by "SEI reaction redirect to LAM":

  • Redirect-to-LAM (the one used for CELL064's Si, current recommended form — current-conserving, reuses the exact reaction current sei_growth.py has already stopped counting toward SEI/LLI):

    jiso = γ · g · (a·jsei) / F · fremain 

    j_iso = redirect_LAM_yield * isolation_gate * a_j_sei / F * remaining_frac
    where a·jsei (a_j_sei) is the SEI volumetric interfacial current density [A/m3], F Faraday's constant, γ (redirect_LAM_yield, "...SEI-redirect-to-LAM yield") the fraction of the redirected current that manifests as LAM (rather than vanishing).
  • Lsei-driven (an alternative form, not the one used for CELL064):

    jiso = −β · Lsei · g · fremain 

    j_iso = -beta_LAM_iso * L_sei * isolation_gate * remaining_frac
    driven by accumulated SEI thickness Lsei (L_sei, a monotonically non-decreasing stock) rather than the instantaneous SEI reaction current, so it does not collapse to 0 once electrolyte access is diffusion-limited — β (beta_LAM_iso) is "...porosity-isolation LAM factor [s-1.m-1]".

Either way, jiso is added into the phase's active-material-fraction ODE:

dεs/dt = ... + jiso 

d(eps_solid)/dt += j_iso

(alongside any stress-driven/reaction-driven LAM terms already present), and the corresponding lithium trapped in the now-isolated material is tracked as lli_due_to_lam (Sulzer et al. 2021 eq. 37's channel) so it counts toward the model's total LLI, not just LAM.

How this fits into core PyBaMM's submodel structure

  • Porosity submodel (src/pybamm/models/submodels/porosity/reaction_driven_porosity.py): computes structural porosity eps_n as a function of accumulated SEI/plating thickness — this is what actually closes as the cell ages, and what headroom_frac above reads.
  • Active-material submodel (loss_active_material.py, above): reads eps_n (from porosity, built earlier in the submodel call order — see build-order note below) and a_j_sei/L_sei (from the SEI submodel) to compute and apply j_iso.
  • SEI submodel (src/pybamm/models/submodels/interface/sei/sei_growth.py): under the redirect-to-LAM option, reduces its own SEI-film-growth rate dcdt_sei by the same isolation_gate fraction, so the redirected current is genuinely moved from "SEI growth" to "LAM", not double-counted.
  • Build-order constraint (discovered/confirmed this session): base_lithium_ion_model.py's set_submodels() registers submodels in a fixed order — particle mechanics (crack/expansion) is registered before porosity and active material. Within one coupled-variables pass, a submodel may only safely read a coupled (non-fundamental) variable from a submodel registered earlier in that list. This means base_mechanics.py (particle mechanics/expansion) cannot read loss_active_material.py's isolation_gate/j_iso — but loss_active_material.py (registered later) can safely read and patch mechanics' already-built "Cell thickness change [m]" output. This ordering constraint governed where several of this session's expansion- related mechanisms could physically be implemented.

Finding 3: BoL solid-diffusion parameterization (particle radius & diffusivity) controls the EoL stoichiometric collapse

The problem, mechanistically

Si's solid-state diffusivity (silicon_LGM50_diffusivity_Bonkile2024, D_ref = 3.0e-16 m2/s) and particle radius (1.52e-6 m) are both literature placeholders carried into CELL064's parameter set unfitted — flagged [LITERATURE - TO FIT] in cell064_parameters.py's own provenance notes, since no high-C-rate pouch data exists to fit them against directly.

As the cell ages, Si loses active material via the porosity-isolation mechanism (Finding 2) — by EFC~200-250 the model's own Si active-volume fraction eps_s can fall to ~5-10% of its BoL value. The same total discharge/charge current is now forced through a much smaller remaining active-Si volume, so the local current density on the surviving particles rises sharply — even at a nominally slow C-rate (a "C/20" RPT can become a locally much higher effective rate once enough Si is isolated). Combined with Si's small particle radius and low diffusivity, the solid-diffusion time constant

tau_diffusion ~ R^2 / D

becomes large relative to the time available for lithium to equilibrate across the remaining particle at this elevated local current density. The result is severe solid-diffusion (concentration) polarisation: the particle surface hits the delithiation/lithiation limit and the terminal voltage crosses the cutoff long before the bulk of the particle has actually been drained/filled — i.e. the model's own particle-surface stoichiometry excursion within a cycle collapses to a tiny sliver, even though the true thermodynamically-accessible capacity is much larger.

Confirmed directly this session: with the OLD (unfitted) diffusivity/radius, the model's own Si stoichiometric window (sto_max - sto_min within one discharge) collapsed ~9-300x from early life to EoL, depending on whether measured on the slow C/20 RPT diagnostic or the faster C/3 ageing cycle. Real CELL064 data shows nothing like this — see the table below.

This directly contradicts the old baseline's ~9-300x collapse: the real cell is nowhere near as diffusion/polarisation-limited at EoL as the unfitted parameters made the model.

The fix and its effect

Wired C64_SI_DIFFUSIVITY_MULT and C64_SI_PARTICLE_RADIUS_MULT (simple scalar multipliers on the literature values) into cell064_degradation_fit.py's PARAM_UPDATES. Particle radius is the stronger lever per unit change (tau ~ R^2/D, quadratic vs. diffusivity's linear effect) — a radius cut to x0.2 of the literature value, combined with a x3 diffusivity increase, was adopted for the new baseline. This relieves the solid-diffusion bottleneck enough that the remaining active Si can actually equilibrate across a full discharge even once most of the phase has been isolated — allowing the model's stoichiometric window to stay close to fully utilised at EoL, matching the real DMA data's ~1.27x (not ~9-300x) collapse.
