# Electrical-Isolation LAM/LLI Coupling — Implementation Plan

**Origin**: CELL064's `test_pathways` investigation (task2/task2j-m). Root-cause chain:
the model's post-knee C/20 discharge voltage sag is a genuine, growing, whole-curve
mismatch (confirmed via direct Q-dependent breakdown, not a timing artifact — user
verified), traced to graphite's active-material window collapsing to its zero floor
within ~20% of post-knee discharge and staying dead for the rest (task2e). That
collapse is driven by excessive **LLI**: the model's own internal state attributes
~74-87% of negative-electrode capacity loss to LLI vs. the real cell's DMA-measured
~40-47% (`CELL064_LLI.csv`), while under-attributing to genuine Si active-material loss
(model LAM_Si ~28-31% vs. real ~58-80%, `CELL064_LAM_derived.csv`). Nine independent
degradation-rate parameters (task2j-m, ~300+ runs) and Si's raw electrochemical kinetics
(particle radius, exchange-current density — tested directly this session) could not
fix this split without either failing to change it or catastrophically breaking knee
timing. Root cause, confirmed by direct code inspection (`loss_active_material.py`):
`SI_BETA_LAM_SEI` — the only existing lever nominally connecting Si's crack-SEI
reaction to active-material loss — is a **purely additive** term
(`j_stress_reaction = beta_LAM_sei * a_j_sei / F`, added to `deps_solid_dt`) that adds
extra LAM *on top of* the SEI reaction's own unchanged lithium consumption, rather than
*redirecting* it — so turning it up only accelerates total degradation, it never
reduces LLI's share. Confirmed a second, compounding effect while scoping this plan:
`loss_active_material.py` already has a *first-principles-correct* piece of exactly
this kind of coupling (`lli_due_to_lam`, Sulzer et al. 2021 eq. 37) that tracks lithium
trapped *in* material as it's lost — meaning every unit of `SI_BETA_LAM_SEI`-driven LAM
also triggers its own LLI hit on top, making the additive-only problem worse, not
better, the harder it's pushed.

**Scope of this plan**: a genuinely new, physically-motivated LAM-generation
mechanism — *progressive electrical isolation of active material under accumulated
crack-SEI coverage* — that is **self-limiting** (isolated material stops reacting
entirely, capping *further* SEI growth/LLI at that site) rather than purely additive,
gated behind a new, off-by-default model option, mirroring how `pore_buffering` and
`"SEI porosity change"` are already opt-in toggles in this codebase.

## 0) Literature check (per instruction, before committing to a design)

Searched for existing treatments of this exact coupling (crack/SEI-driven electrical
isolation as a distinct LAM channel from bulk lithium trapping) — findings:

- **Electrical (not just chemical) isolation is a well-documented, named mechanism** for
  Si and Si-Gr composite anodes specifically: "repeated expansion and contraction can
  induce microcracking, binder fatigue, pore-structure collapse, and loss of Si–Si,
  Si–conductive additive, and particle–current collector contact, leading to
  electrically isolated Si domains" and "Si-C particles can remain mechanically intact
  but become electrically disconnected due to the formation of a thick, resistive SEI
  generated by repeated electrolyte decomposition" — [Chemical and mechanical
  degradation and mitigation strategies for Si anodes, J. Power Sources
  2019](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S037877531930179X);
  [Imaging of electric failure in Si-alloy/graphite-blended anodes, J. Power Sources
  2020](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0378775320315998). This
  is exactly the "SEI clogs the crack surface until the fragment stops conducting"
  mechanism this plan proposes, described independently in the literature.
- **A PyBaMM-native precedent exists for exactly this composite Si-Gr degradation
  question**: ["Is silicon worth it? Modelling degradation in composite
  silicon–graphite lithium-ion battery electrodes", J. Power Sources
  2024](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0378775324002076)
  ([PDF](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/380066284_Is_silicon_worth_it_Modelling_degradation_in_composite_silicon-graphite_lithium-ion_battery_electrodes))
  — built directly in PyBaMM, couples multiple negative-electrode degradation
  mechanisms and reports on the LLI/LAM balance for Si-Gr blends. Worth reading in full
  before finalizing the equation form below — it may already have solved the
  attribution question a different way, or use compatible variable names.
- **DMA-based LAM_Si/LAM_Gr separation methodology precedent**: ["Assessment of the
  Impact of Silicon on Loss of Active Material Quantification in Blended
  Silicon/Graphite Negative Electrodes", J. Electrochem. Soc.
  2025](https://iopscience.iop.org/article/10.1149/1945-7111/adfca1) — directly
  relevant to validating whatever this plan implements against `CELL064_LAM_derived.csv`
  the same way the real DMA numbers were separated.
- **A full electrochemical-mechanical Si-Gr coupled model with crack-driven SEI and
  LAM, validated against LG M50T data**: ["Coupled electrochemical-thermal-mechanical
  stress modelling in composite silicon/graphite lithium-ion battery
  electrodes"](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S2352152X23020066)
  and a related decoupling-analysis paper: ["Decoupling analysis of degradation
  mechanism in silicon–graphite lithium-ion batteries with
  electrochemical-thermal-mechanical-aging coupling
  model"](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S001346862600873X) —
  describes three separate pathways (natural SEI growth, crack-induced SEI growth, LAM
  with particle cracking) rather than one additive term; worth checking whether they
  keep these three pathways as independent lithium sinks (additive, like this model
  currently does) or route lithium between them (the redistribution this plan wants).
- **Not found**: a published closed-form equation for "fraction of crack-SEI reaction
  current that manifests as isolation vs. continued film growth" — i.e., no paper
  gives a ready-made formula to drop in; the mechanism is qualitatively well-supported
  but the specific coupling below is this plan's own construction, to be validated
  against CELL064's own DMA data (§4), not lifted from a source.
- **Action before implementing**: read the two PyBaMM-based papers above in full
  (especially the 2024 "Is silicon worth it?" one, since it may share code lineage
  with this repo's `si_gr_expansion.py` parameter set) to check whether they've already
  built and published the redistribution mechanism this plan proposes, which would
  mean adapting their approach rather than designing from scratch.

## 1) The mechanism, precisely

**Core idea**: once a fractured particle surface has accumulated enough SEI (i.e. once
`{phase} SEI on cracks thickness [m]` has grown past some threshold), treat a fraction
of that surface's electrochemically-active area as **permanently electrically
isolated** — it stops carrying reaction current (no more charge/discharge current
sharing, no more SEI growth) but the lithium it was holding at the moment of isolation
becomes trapped, via the *existing, already-correct* `lli_due_to_lam` mechanism (no new
LLI bookkeeping needed — reuse it).

Why this is **self-limiting**, unlike `beta_LAM_sei`: once area is isolated, it can no
longer react at all, so it stops both drawing charge/discharge current *and* growing
more SEI. That directly reduces the *future* electrochemically-active crack area,
which is what should eventually slow down (not endlessly accelerate) the whole
crack-SEI-driven degradation channel — a genuine negative feedback the current
formulation lacks entirely.

Why this should leave **existing, already-tuned dynamics alone** at low isolation
rates: at BoL and pre-knee, `L_sei_cr_k` (accumulated crack-SEI thickness) is small, so
the isolated fraction is ~0 — this mechanism only engages once crack-SEI has built up
meaningfully, i.e. specifically in the post-knee regime this investigation is about,
not diluting the already-good pre-knee fit (RPT1/RPT2, which task2m's search and the
formation-rate fix already got close).

## 3) Proposed equations and new parameters

In `loss_active_material.py`'s `"reaction" in lam_option` branch, alongside (not
necessarily replacing) the existing `j_stress_reaction` term:

```python
if "reaction" in lam_option:
    beta_LAM_sei = self.phase_param.beta_LAM_sei
    a_j_sei = variables[...]  # unchanged, existing lookup
    j_stress_reaction = beta_LAM_sei * a_j_sei / self.param.F
    deps_solid_dt += j_stress_reaction

    # NEW: electrical isolation of cracked, SEI-clogged surface area.
    # Gated behind a new option value so this is fully opt-in.
    if "isolation" in lam_option:
        L_sei_cr = variables[f"{Domain} {phase_name}SEI on cracks thickness [m]"]
        L_iso_thresh = self.phase_param.L_isolation_threshold   # NEW parameter
        k_iso = self.phase_param.k_isolation                     # NEW parameter [s-1]
        # Smooth onset (sigmoid, not a hard if/else -- avoids a solver-hostile
        # kink, same philosophy as the exponent_max_sei tanh cap and the
        # porosity-floor softplus already in this codebase) once accumulated
        # crack-SEI thickness passes the threshold.
        isolation_rate = k_iso * pybamm.sigmoid(
            (L_sei_cr - L_iso_thresh) / L_iso_thresh, 10.0  # sharpness constant, tune
        )
        deps_solid_dt += -isolation_rate  # separate from j_stress_reaction,
                                           # NOT multiplied by a_j_sei -- this is
                                           # the key structural difference: driven
                                           # by ACCUMULATED SEI state, not
                                           # INSTANTANEOUS reaction current, so it
                                           # doesn't automatically scale up
                                           # whenever SI_CRACK_MULT is pushed
                                           # harder for knee-timing reasons.
```

**Two new phase-level parameters** (Si/`Secondary:` only, per §2):
- `"{pref}Isolation threshold thickness [m]"` (`L_isolation_threshold`) — the
  accumulated crack-SEI thickness above which isolation engages. Physically: how much
  SEI has to clog a crack before the fragment loses electrical contact. No literature
  value found (§0) — start as a free-but-bounded fit parameter, order of magnitude
  comparable to `L_sei_cr0`/existing crack-SEI thicknesses observed in current runs
  (check the model's own `L_sei_cr_k` trajectory at RPT4 in the current best point
  first, to pick a sensible starting bound).
- `"{pref}Isolation rate constant [s-1]"` (`k_isolation`) — how fast isolation
  proceeds once triggered. This is the parameter that should end up doing most of the
  work in the validation fit (§4) — it directly controls how much of the post-knee
  degradation gets redirected from LLI into LAM_Si.

**Critically, do NOT modify `sei_growth.py`'s own `dcdt_sei` or `j_sei` for the
isolated fraction in the first pass** — i.e., don't try to reduce future SEI growth by
reducing the *reacting area* for the isolated fraction yet (that's the "genuinely
self-limiting" refinement mentioned in §1, and it's real, but it requires threading a
correction through the crack-area/roughness calculation shared with the SEI growth
term, which risks destabilizing the well-tuned existing SEI/porosity dynamics). Land
the additive isolation term first, get it validated against the DMA split (§4), *then*
revisit whether the self-limiting feedback is worth the added complexity — flagged
explicitly as a deferred follow-up, same pattern as how the pore-buffering plan
deferred its own §3/§4 pressure-coupling phase.

## 4) Parameterisation / validation procedure

Unlike the pore-buffering plan (which used only BoL data), this mechanism is
inherently a **post-knee, degraded-state** effect, so it needs the CELL064 DMA data
already in-repo as ground truth:

1. **Target values** (already available, no new data collection needed):
   `CELL064_LAM_derived.csv` (LAM_Si ≈ 58.5% @ RPT4 EFC171, ≈ 79.6% @ RPT5 EFC197) and
   `CELL064_LLI.csv` (LLI ≈ 40% @ RPT4, ≈ 47% @ RPT5) — the direct model-internal
   readout convention already established this session (`LAM_gr`/`LAM_si`/`LAM_pos`
   read directly, `LLI = Q_side / NOMINAL_CAP_AH`, **not** the degenerate pOCV-probe
   proxy in `task1_bol_pathway_fit.py`, which the user already correctly flagged as
   unreliable for this comparison).
2. **Fix everything else** at the current best validated combined config
   (`si_crack=0.3375, si_ksei=1.5e-3, gr_ksei=1.333e-4, si_beta_lam_sei=1e-5,
   si_crit_stress=2.2e8, gr_crit_stress=3.0e7, si_lam_prop=3.078e-8,
   porosity_floor=0.033, exp_max_sei=120`, plus the formation-rate fix) as the starting
   point, so the already-good knee timing (`knee_err=1.4`) isn't re-broken by touching
   unrelated knobs while validating the new mechanism in isolation.
3. **Small grid/compass search over just the 2 new parameters**
   (`L_isolation_threshold`, `k_isolation`), reusing `cell064_sweep_lib.py`'s existing
   `launch`/`parse_scores` machinery (same convention as `task2m_pattern_search.py`),
   scoring against: (a) the DMA-target LAM_Si/LLI split at RPT4/RPT5 (NEW score
   function needed — a small addition to `cell064_sweep_lib.py` or
   `cell064_degradation_fit.py`, `score_lam_lli_split()`, computing `|LAM_si_model -
   LAM_si_real| + |LLI_model - LLI_real|` at each matched RPT), (b) confirm
   `knee_efc_err` doesn't regress from the current ~1.4, and (c) confirm
   `voltage_shape_rmse` — the metric that actually matters, per the user's explicit
   standing instruction this session that curve shape, not aggregate capacity/expansion
   fit, is the real test of whether this is "fixed" vs. overfit — improves
   substantially, ideally toward the sub-100mV territory already reached but this time
   validated by a *correct* mechanism rather than a compressed/co-incidentally-tuned
   parameter point.
4. **Re-run the full plot suite** (`plot_all`, as already done for the electrolyte
   check) at the winning point and visually confirm the LAM/LLI panel and RPT-voltage
   overlay both look right — not just the summary RMSE numbers, per the direct
   instruction that visual inspection is the real bar (the electrolyte-concentration
   test this session is the cautionary example: RMSE improved, but the SoH/LLI panel
   looked worse on inspection).

## 5) What NOT to do in this phase / open questions

- **Don't extend this to graphite** without a separate justification — task2e already
  showed `LAM_Gr` stays small (matching reality) in every diagnostic; this mechanism
  is specifically about Si's known electrical-isolation failure mode from the
  literature (§0), not a generic "add isolation everywhere" change.
- **Don't couple the isolated fraction back into the reacting area (`a`/roughness) yet**
  — the genuinely self-limiting refinement (§3's note) is real and probably needed
  eventually to fully realize the "caps runaway LLI" benefit, but land the simpler
  additive-LAM-only version first and validate it against the DMA split before adding
  that complexity.
- **Open question, needs the literature papers in §0 read in full before finalizing**:
  do the "Is silicon worth it?"/ETMA papers already implement (and publish parameter
  values for) something equivalent? If so, adapting a published, peer-reviewed
  formulation and parameter set is strictly better than this plan's from-scratch
  sigmoid-threshold construction.
- **Open question**: should `k_isolation` itself depend on temperature or C-rate (as
  `beta_LAM_sei`/`stress_critical` implicitly do via the variables they read)? Deferred
  — start temperature/rate-independent, revisit only if the DMA-split fit in §4 can't
  be closed without it.
- **Numerical stability**: the sigmoid sharpness constant (hardcoded `10.0` above) will
  need the same kind of care as `exponent_max_sei`'s tanh cap — too sharp risks the
  same solver failures documented throughout this project's TUNING STATUS; start soft
  and sharpen only if the transition needs to be crisper to match the DMA data's own
  RPT-to-RPT jump.
